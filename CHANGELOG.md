## [Unreleased]

### Added
- Compact lexicon record storage (`engine/src/lexicon.rs`): the loaded lexicon is no longer a `Vec<LexiconEntry>` (200 B per entry plus nine heap allocations) but a `LexiconStore` of flat arrays: one text arena for word, normalized form and lemma (a form equal to another form of the same entry is stored once), per-entry spans, parallel frequency and frequency-metadata arrays, and `u32` ids into interned status, part-of-speech, region and source tables (72 B per entry; the allocation count scales with the number of distinct interned categorical values rather than with the number of entries, 49 allocations for the current experimental-full store). The pack decoder borrows strings from the payload instead of allocating them, and next-word context lookup uses the query index's binary search (prediction p50 on experimental-full: bigram 21.8 → 0.75 µs, trigram 75.7 → 1.29 µs). Experimental-full (42,422 entries): engine heap 25.48 → 15.73 MB (601 → 371 B/entry), lexicon structures 13.53 → 3.77 MB and 381,799 → 49 allocations, RSS after load 51.3 → 38.7 MB, load 118 → 109 ms; reviewed: heap 1.56 → 1.24 MB. Pack format and bytes unchanged; golden query JSON, the 357-case comparison and the query-index/reference equivalence suite are byte-identical or passing; no ABI, SDK, ranking or prediction change. `Engine::load_lexicon(Vec<LexiconEntry>)` is unchanged, `FrequencyMetadata` derives `Copy`, the store is crate-private (no new public API), every offset and id is a checked `u32` so a lexicon beyond the representation fails closed (`PackLoadError::InvalidPayload` for packs, a named panic for `load_lexicon`), and `memory_attribution` reports the store's arrays. Details in `docs/memory-attribution.md`.
- ku-Latn orthography contract and keyboard requirements (`docs/ku-latn-keyboard.md`): `data/keyboard/ku-Latn-orthography.json` (normative, `ku-latn-orthography-v1`) records the locale tag, the 31-letter alphabet exactly as the default-pack alphabet policy defines it, the five distinct letters `ç ê î ş û` with code points and decompositions, Unicode-default casing pairs with the explicit rule that no other locale's dotted or dotless i casing may be applied, the engine's normalization contract, and what the contract deliberately does not cover (word-internal hyphen and apostrophes, digits and symbols, characters observed in source data). `data/keyboard/ku-Latn-keyboard-requirements.json` (`ku-latn-keyboard-requirements-v1`) states vendor-neutral capability requirements: all 31 letters typeable in both cases, the access mechanism for the five letters left to the vendor (no long-press or dedicated-key prescription), distinct-letter identity (`ç ê î ş û` are distinct letters, not interchangeable representations of `c e i s u`, for storage, normalization, comparison, casing and lexical identity, while spell correction and diacritic restoration may still propose a base-letter ↔ distinct-letter change as a linguistic correction), casing, encoding, locale tag, host-owned non-lexical layers; the physical arrangement is `intentionally-unspecified` by explicit project decision (2026-09-17): shipping Apple, Gboard and Samsung keyboards all type the 31 letters but differ in arrangement and in access to `ç ê î ş û` (dedicated keys, dedicated keys, long press), documented as evidence levels in `docs/evaluation/ku-latn-platform-keyboard-inspection-2026-09-17.md` and as `observed_implementations` in the file, never as requirements; `layout_ku.json` is named as the engine's key-adjacency evidence, not a layout authority, and its re-evaluation against vendor layouts is a separate future engine task. Both files are `draft-pending-human-review`; the change introduces no new linguistic decision (the orthography contract codifies the existing human-approved 31-letter alphabet policy; tooling makes no linguistic decisions). `ku_latn_contracts_test.rs` enforces the strict schemas, the alphabet's identity with the policy, distinct-letter and casing consistency with Unicode and the engine, that the requirements demand exactly the alphabet and prescribe no mechanism or foreign letter, that the identity requirement does not forbid the engine's existing diacritic correction (normalization never maps `i` to `î`; the correction API still returns the human-reviewed `biji` → `bijî` case), adjacency-evidence consistency, and that the seed and reviewed vocabularies need nothing beyond the alphabet.
- Default-pack alphabet policy (`data-builder/src/alphabet.rs`, `docs/lexicon-review.md`): an explicit human project policy that a lexical entry of the default/reviewed pack consists only of the 31 letters of the Kurmancî alphabet (`a b c ç d e ê f g h i î j k l m n o p q r s ş t u û v w x y z`), applied mechanically to the normalized form. Digits, superscripts and symbols are not eligible; alphabetic characters outside the alphabet are not eligible; hyphen and apostrophes are deliberately left to a separate human-reviewed tokenization policy. One reusable eligibility function (`default_pack_eligibility`) is enforced in three places: before ordinary review, where `generate-review-queues` keeps violating Hunspell import entries out of the review pool and writes them to a new `alphabet-policy-excluded.jsonl` evidence queue (`ALPHABET_POLICY_V1`, `OUT_OF_ALPHABET`, `excluded_by_alphabet_policy`; import untouched, identities unchanged, hyphen/apostrophe forms stay reviewable) and the corpus technical filter classifies such tokens as `out_of_alphabet` so they never enter a Kuwiki review batch; at authoritative resolution, where `pack::selection::apply_default_pack_alphabet_policy` runs once over the merged candidates of every source and fails closed (`production lexical eligibility violation`, naming form, source and decision) when an approved, metadata-change or seed candidate violates the policy, so `build-pack`, `verify-production-state`, `rebuild-production` and the release bundle refuse until the decision artifact is corrected; and at the default-vocabulary boundary, where `validate_entry` (manual seed lexicon, metadata-change replacements) keeps its structural checks but takes its character rule from the same function instead of its own list (a space is therefore no longer accepted there). Undecided or experimental-only evidence in experimental-full is not gated. Data correction under the policy: the 13 Kuwiki candidates approved although outside the alphabet (`20an`, `2012an`, `2015an`, `comté`, `côte`, `hérault`, `héraultê`, `isère`, `km²`, `più`, `saône`, `sèvres`, `württemberg`) are now `rejected_from_default_pack` in `data/review-decisions/kuwiki-batch-001` (12) and `kuwiki-batch-002` (1), each note stating the policy basis, the offending characters and the previous decision; batch manifests, artifact hashes, README counts and the pinned constants follow; the Hunspell review queues are regenerated (95 entries excluded from the ordinary pool, 41,403 remain) and the committed vocabulary review batch report (`data/reports/vocabulary-review/top-1000.*`, derived from that pool) with them; source imports and batches are untouched. `rebuild-production` regenerated the language model `kuwiki-20260801` (vocabulary 42,433 → 42,420, unigrams 14,392 → 14,379, bigrams 47,700 → 47,584, trigrams 58,841 → 58,627) and the packs: reviewed 1,464 → 1,451 entries, experimental-full 42,435 → 42,422, seed unchanged; `verify-production-state` passes, packs are byte-identical on a second build, and the 357-case pack comparison changes only two reviewed suggestion lists (`più` and `km²` no longer appear) with every verdict and count unchanged. `verify-production-state` now also fails closed on the n-gram configuration: a committed language model built with a different `data-builder/config/ngrams.toml`, or a missing or unreadable configuration file, fails the language-model check. New `data-builder audit-alphabet [--json]` writes a lightweight, diagnostic-only report (decisions admitting an out-of-alphabet form, Hunspell pool and excluded-queue counts, Kuwiki batch inventory, packs) under `data/reports/alphabet-audit/` and modifies nothing; nothing it lists is a keyboard requirement. Tests: `alphabet_policy_test.rs` (rule, casing and decomposed input, fail-closed gate for Kuwiki, Hunspell and a future source, legitimate reservoir evidence, corrected repository, audit no-write guarantee, no Kuwiki approval outside the alphabet), `alphabet_policy_review_test.rs` (Hunspell pool excludes violating entries and keeps evidence with the import unchanged; an approved Hunspell decision on such an entry fails authoritative resolution while a rejection resolves), `vocabulary_evidence_test.rs` (corpus tokens outside the alphabet never reach the eligible review queue).
- Query-time candidate index (`engine/src/index.rs`): `suggest`, `correct` and `complete` no longer scan every lexicon entry. The engine builds at load a sorted normalized-form index (binary search instead of the linear lookup per trie prefix hit) and a second compact trie over diacritic-stripped forms, walked with a unit-cost optimal-string-alignment bound of 2 (`Trie::fuzzy_terminals`). The walk yields a proven superset of every entry the scorer can accept (every non-diacritic edit costs at least 0.75, so at most two fit under the 2.0 threshold; proof in the module), and the candidates are re-scored by the same stage functions the reference uses, in ascending lexicon order, so results, kinds, edit costs, ordering and counts are byte-identical. The full scan is retained crate-privately as `Engine::suggest_reference_full_scan` as the oracle; `query_equivalence_tests` brute-forces the pruning lemma, asserts the superset invariant and compares the ranked output on a synthetic lexicon and on the reviewed and experimental packs over benchmark and QA inputs, every evaluation-suite input, a deterministic near-miss corpus and Unicode edge cases (the real-pack suite runs in release mode as its own CI step; `KURMANCI_EQUIVALENCE_SCALE=full` for the wide local run). Experimental-full latency (release, p50): suggest 37 ms → 0.35 ms, correct 28 ms → 0.19 ms, complete 33 ms → 0.64 ms; the index costs 3.15 MB heap and 47 ms of load on that pack, reported by `memory_attribution()` and the bench (`query index` rows). No pack, model, ranking, prediction, ABI or SDK change; the 357-case pack comparison is byte-identical. Details in `docs/memory-attribution.md`.
- Android clean-room proof: CI now builds the AAR (Rust + cargo-ndk) in `Android AAR + Emulator Consumer` and uploads it, and a separate `Android Consumer Clean-Room (Rust-Free)` job, with the runner's Rust removed from PATH and no NDK installed, downloads the AAR into the local Maven layout and runs `scripts/android/verify-clean-room-consumer.sh`: it fails if `cargo`, `rustc` or `cargo-ndk` is reachable, requires the packaged `.aar`/`.pom`, checks that the AAR carries `libkurmanci_jni.so` for every supported ABI and records its SHA-256, builds the standalone Kotlin consumer with `CONSUMER_MODE=local`, runs its JVM unit tests and the emulator instrumentation, including the new `testCleanRoomContractKnownCorrectCompletePredict` (load pack, known, correct, complete, predict through the public Kotlin API). No SDK or architecture change; `android/README.md` documents the verification.
- Device benchmark harness (`docs/device-benchmark.md`): `DeviceBenchmarkTests` in the iOS consumer test host and `DeviceBenchmarkTest` in the Android consumer record load time, resident memory after load and after queries, p50/p95/max latency of known / suggest / correct / complete / predict over 200 calls, and repeated-query stability over 300 rounds (the only assertion), as one `KURMANCI_DEVICE_BENCHMARK` JSON line. `scripts/apple/device-benchmark.sh` and `scripts/android/device-benchmark.sh` swap a real pack into the committed placeholder `benchmark_pack.bin`, run the harness on a simulator/emulator or a real device (`--destination`, `XCODEBUILD_EXTRA_ARGS` for signing), restore the placeholder, and write the report to `dist/device-benchmarks/`. The reports carry the measured pack's SHA-256 (`pack_sha256`) with its bytes, entry count and format version; a failed XCTest or instrumentation run (the stability assertion included) exits non-zero and writes no report, the Apple wrapper taking xcodebuild's status from `PIPESTATUS` and keeping the failed log, which `scripts/apple/test-device-benchmark-wrapper.sh` verifies with a fake xcodebuild in CI. CI runs the harness with the placeholder as a smoke test; no timing gate.
- Deterministic release bundle: `data-builder build-release-bundle [--out DIR] [--release-version V] [--apple PATH]... [--android PATH]... [--allow-dirty] [--json]` refuses unless `verify-production-state` passes and every pack is built and current, then assembles `kurmanci-ku-Latn-<version>/` in memory and installs it by failure-safe staged replacement: `VERSION`, `compatibility.json` (engine version, C ABI major/minor read from `kurmanci.h`, pack magic and schemas, language-model schema, language tag), `provenance.json` (source commit and `data/` tree id, toolchain, every pack's artifact hashes and review provenance, the language model's hashes, corpus provenance and licensing block with `redistribution_determination` left as recorded, registered sources and corpora, SPDX identifiers and per-subject redistribution records, attached platform artifacts, and the full production-state report), `SHA256SUMS` over every other file, `ATTRIBUTION`, `LICENSES/`, `include/kurmanci.h`, `packs/<id>/` (all five artifacts of the three packs) and `language-model/<id>/`. The bundle is labelled `evaluation` unless every redistribution determination is `allowed`; no legal determination is made. Apple/Android artifacts from the existing scripts are attached and hashed as received. `verify-release-bundle DIR` re-checks a bundle read-only (every listed file with its hash, no unlisted file, provenance/compatibility/VERSION/manifests consistent). `scripts/release/verify-clean-checkout-determinism.sh` clones the repository twice, derives the Hunspell import, quality audit, review queues, validated decisions and packs in each, builds a bundle in each and fails unless they are byte-identical; CI runs it as a parallel job. `docs/RELEASE_PROVENANCE.md` describes the bundle, the data, the review process, the licensing state and the reproducibility claims. `dist/` is now ignored. Replacing an existing bundle is a failure-safe staged replacement (stage, park the previous bundle as a backup, rename, restore on failure, and restore a backup that is the only copy of the previous release before any new attempt; `release::install_directory`); the cleanliness check counts untracked non-ignored files as dirty because `NOTICE` and `data/licenses/` are bundle inputs; recursive traversal never follows symbolic links and fails naming the offending path, for supplied artifact trees and for verified bundles alike; a bundle built with `--allow-dirty` is always an evaluation release with a notice, and `verify-release-bundle` independently enforces that a dirty tree cannot be `production`, that the release kind follows from the recorded determinations and dirty state, that an evaluation release carries a notice, and that the language-model schema version agrees between `compatibility.json` and `provenance.json`.
- `data-builder verify-production-state [--json]`: read-only, fail-closed verification of the whole production state in one command. Authoritative inputs: source and corpus registries, pack policy, Hunspell queue/report manifests and decisions (no orphans), Kuwiki batch artifacts with cross-batch no-repeat and no context references, decision identities unique across every source, seed ⊆ reviewed ⊆ experimental-full, and a pack-policy language-model set that is exactly what `rebuild-production` generates from the registered corpora. Derived outputs: the committed language model (artifact hashes, content invariants, vocabulary fingerprint, registry provenance, and the local canonical import/partition when it includes the model's corpus), every pack's complete artifact set (`lexicon.bin`, `manifest.json`, `collision-report.jsonl`, `attribution.txt`, `artifacts.sha256`) assembled twice in memory through the same path `build-pack` writes (`pack::assemble_pack_artifacts`) and compared file by file with the built pack, so stale attribution, licensing or provenance metadata fails even when the lexicon bytes are unchanged; built pack manifests; local corpus pipeline consistency. Every check is listed as pass, fail or skipped with a reason; the exit status is non-zero when any check fails; nothing is written; diagnostics never slice ids or hashes. `data-builder rebuild-production [--acquire] [--json]`: the deliberate counterpart. It first verifies the authoritative inputs read-only and refuses, changing nothing, when any of them fails; then re-imports and re-partitions only when the local corpus state is missing or stale, rebuilds the committed language model with the one production configuration (`PRODUCTION_LANGUAGE_MODEL_BUILD`, also the `build-language-model` defaults) and all packs from the committed human decisions, validates, verifies, and reports the hash of every regenerated artifact as changed, unchanged or new. It fails without changes when the Kuwiki corpus is absent unless `--acquire` is given (the only path that downloads), and never assigns, changes or promotes a decision. Progress lines of the pack builder, corpus importer, partitioner and acquirer now go to stderr, so the `--json` output of both commands is exactly one JSON document on stdout.
- Concurrency, leak and Unicode hardening tests: `engine/tests/concurrency_unicode_test.rs` (16 threads on one engine answer exactly the single-threaded results; Send/Sync; precomposed, decomposed and cased forms of ç ê î ş û query the same word; BOM, zero-width space and control characters are removed by the canonical rule so decorated words match; empty and mixed input), `engine/tests/leak_test.rs` (100,000 mixed queries leave the live heap unchanged under a counting allocator), `ffi/tests/boundary_test.rs` (C answers equal core answers for 46 Unicode inputs; six malformed UTF-8 encodings rejected by every function with `KMR_ERROR_INVALID_ARGUMENT`; 12 threads on one handle; destroy-after-callers rule) and `ffi/tests/leak_test.rs` (create/query/destroy cycles and 20,000 query cycles return the heap to baseline). JNI bridge: panics in the query bridges now throw `NativeException` instead of returning null, invalid words throw `InvalidArgumentException`, per-element local references are released while building result arrays, and a null context array is an empty context. Swift and Android instrumentation tests gain decomposed-input parity, a close-during-queries race and stress loops. Engine runtime normalization now applies the repository's canonical rule (`normalize_text`: control characters, U+200B and U+FEFF removed, then NFC and lower case) so runtime lookups match pack words and review identities exactly; the Kotlin `isKnownWord` no longer rejects empty input and returns false like every other surface. `docs/integration.md` gains input-handling and threading contracts.

- `data-builder inspect-word WORD [--json]`: read-only inspector for the review workflow. For the canonical review identity of a word (`normalize_text`) it reports pack membership from the authoritative resolver (seed / reviewed / experimental-full, display forms, pack sources), source evidence (Hunspell import entries and source lines, TRAIN-partition statistics from the committed language model, Kuwiki review-batch candidate statistics) and the human review history per source (Hunspell entry and conflict-group targets, Kuwiki batch targets, each with status, reviewer, date and notes, `pending` when undecided). Statuses are never flattened; nothing is assigned, inferred, promoted or written; no corpus text, document ids or context references are read or shown.
- Runtime QA CLI (`kurmanci-cli`): new `known`, `correct`, `complete` and `predict` commands and an `interactive` shell over the same handlers, a global `--pack` (any built pack) and `--json` (stable, deterministic output with the engine's own fields: text, kind, edit_cost; text, source, count, probability_millionths). The existing `suggest` and `predict-next` commands and their output are unchanged. The CLI is an adapter over the public `KurmanciEngine` API only: no ranking, prediction, pack or data changes, and no provenance or review data. Tests compare every command's JSON with direct engine results on a schema-4 fixture.
- Engine memory attribution and engineering benchmark: `KurmanciEngine::memory_attribution()` (and `Engine::memory_attribution()`) report a read-only per-structure heap estimate (lexicon records, per-entry strings and vectors, trie child tables and word copies, bigram/trigram tables and lists); the new `kurmanci-bench` crate loads a pack under a counting allocator and reports steady-state versus peak load heap, temporary load allocations, RSS, per-structure attribution and p50/p95/p99 query latency with transient peaks, in text or JSON. ### Changed
- Engine completion trie is now a compact flat-array trie (`engine/src/trie.rs`): one label, first-child index, child count and terminal index per node (16 bytes) instead of a hash table per node, word text stored once, children in code point order; tries are built explicitly after loading. Observable behaviour is unchanged and proven: a reference copy of the previous implementation is compared on every prefix of every word (`trie::equivalence_tests`, including the built packs), 756 golden CLI queries on the reviewed and experimental-full packs are byte-identical, and the 357-case `evaluate-packs` reports are byte-identical. Experimental-full engine heap 58.0 MB → 22.4 MB (trie 39 MB → 3 MB, RSS after load 77 MB → 46 MB), reviewed 2.84 MB → 1.45 MB; see `docs/memory-attribution.md`. `MemoryAttribution` reports `trie_node_arrays` and `trie_words` in place of `trie_child_tables` and `trie_word_copies`; the trie is built and enumerated with explicit stacks, so load and query depth never depend on the call stack (a valid pack may contain a word of tens of thousands of characters).
